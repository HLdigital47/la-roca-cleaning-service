Different office routines need different planning

Private professional suite

Identify work areas, business hours and surfaces that need to remain available for cleaning.

Shared workplace

Distinguish each occupant’s areas from shared responsibilities. Decide who communicates priorities.

Customer-facing office

Consider reception, visitor restrooms and peak foot traffic when organizing the cleaning order.

What to clean and what to leave undisturbed

Available surfaces make cleaning easier; clear instructions help keep important items in place.

Documents and personal belongings

Secure confidential information and mark items to keep. Do not let useful papers or boxes be confused with waste.

Computers and equipment

Identify equipment that should not be handled. Agree on any exterior surface cleaning in advance and follow manufacturer instructions.

Restricted rooms

Identify archives, technical rooms and offices that must stay closed. Define who can authorize entry if availability changes.

Review the service using more than a general impression

Use the agreed list and review the same areas after each visit. This helps communicate specific details.

  • Check areas that were available for cleaning.
  • Review tasks planned for this visit rather than another date.
  • Identify whether staff or visitors used the space after cleaning.
  • For a missed detail, record the area, date and a photo of the specific spot.

When to revisit the plan

Hybrid working

When attendance changes by day, review where use is concentrated rather than assuming every area needs the same frequency.

More staff or visitors

Review restrooms, kitchenette and circulation as occupancy increases. Size may stay the same while the work changes.

Events or new rooms

Share unusual meetings, newly opened areas or layout changes so scope and timing can be assessed before the next visit.

Office cleaning planning checklist

Situations that may arise between visits

A room was locked during cleaning. Is it automatically handled later?

Explain which room was unavailable and when it can be opened. Ask how it can be incorporated into the next visit or whether separate coordination is needed. Do not assume a return outside the agreed appointment is confirmed.

Several businesses share the office. Who should give instructions?

Designate a contact for common areas and confirm which rooms belong to each business. That contact can consolidate priorities and permissions to avoid conflicting instructions during the visit.

Our office manager changed. What should the new contact receive?

Pass along the current task list, schedule, restricted areas, access instructions and La Roca contact details. Review building entry authorizations and confirm who will approve scope or date changes.

There is an unmarked box beside a bin. How do we avoid it being discarded?

Clearly identify boxes to keep and establish a separate point for materials intended for removal. Communicate the rule to the service contact; proximity to a bin should not be the only signal for deciding what is waste.

There was a leak or equipment spilled liquid. Can the usual visit handle it?

First notify the building or equipment contact to control the source. Tell La Roca what happened and which areas were affected so the work can be assessed before treating it as an ordinary upkeep task.
