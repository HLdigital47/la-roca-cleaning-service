Apartment-cleaning questions that affect your estimate

Does a small apartment always need a short cleaning visit?

Not necessarily. A small apartment with a heavily used kitchen, shower buildup or lots of belongings may need substantial detail work. Describe its condition and bathroom count as well as its size. A studio's floor area alone is not enough to estimate the work.

What should I tell the team about stairs, parking and building access?

During follow-up, give the unit, floor, permitted parking and gate or office check-in instructions. Mention elevator rules and any time limits for service providers. Agree on who opens the unit so an appointment is not lost to an access problem.

Are dishes, laundry and closet organization included in apartment cleaning?

Treat them as separate requests. Washing dishes and clothes or sorting storage takes time beyond cleaning accessible surfaces. Clear the sink, floors and counters where possible, and ask whether any extra help can be added to the agreed scope before the visit.

Should I request regular apartment cleaning or move-out cleaning?

Choose based on whether you still live there and what needs access. Regular cleaning maintains occupied rooms; move-out cleaning needs an empty-unit scope aligned with the property instructions. Mention cabinet and appliance interiors and any separate carpet requirement before comparing quotes.
