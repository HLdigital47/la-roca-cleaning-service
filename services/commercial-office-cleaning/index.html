SCOPE BY AREA

Different areas need different attention

Agree with La Roca on what happens during each visit and which details are scheduled separately. The scope should reflect how your space is used, not just its size.

Reception and waiting areas

Cleaning accessible surfaces, side tables and entry areas. Reception is part of the first impression visitors have of your business.

Workstations

Dust removal and cleaning of clear surfaces within the agreed scope. Identify equipment, documents and belongings that should stay in place.

Meeting rooms

Tables, shared surfaces and floors. Consider the meeting calendar so rooms are available when your team needs them.

Restrooms

Cleaning toilets, sinks, faucets, mirrors and floors. The number of users helps determine the attention each restroom requires.

Kitchenette and break areas

Counters, sinks, tables and accessible cabinet or appliance exteriors. Specifically agree on any appliance interiors that need cleaning.

Floors and circulation

Vacuuming or mopping suited to the material, with attention to entrances and hallways. Include bins and agreed touchpoints on your task list.

What shapes your cleaning quote

Two offices with the same square footage can need different work. To prepare a useful proposal, we review:

  • Size, layout and number of restrooms
  • Staff, visitors and common area use
  • Initial condition and requested tasks
  • Frequency, scheduling and access requirements

A PRACTICAL PLAN

Choose frequency around actual use

A quiet professional suite and a busy customer-facing office do not collect dirt at the same rate. These factors help you discuss a suitable plan; frequency is confirmed with La Roca.

Higher traffic

If customers arrive throughout the day or many people share restrooms and a kitchenette, discuss more frequent attention for the busiest areas.

Moderate use

When activity is concentrated on certain days, arrange visits around occupancy and meeting room use.

Periodic details

Separate routine upkeep from detailed tasks. Schedule shelves, agreed interiors and less-used areas while keeping everyday needs in view.

VISIT COORDINATION

Plan cleaning around your operation

Before the first visit, identify areas that are available and those that need a specific time. Share building rules and confirm access with the responsible person.

Timing and circulation

Explain when activity is lower and which hallways need to remain available. Request the time you need and confirm availability before scheduling.

Entry and lock-up

Agree on authorized access, alarm instructions and who verifies lock-up. Keep a reachable contact available during the visit.

Task list and follow-up

Define tasks by area and a contact for changes. An agreed list provides clear criteria for reviewing the service.

Questions from office and business managers

We have a meeting or event on cleaning day. How should we coordinate it?

Let La Roca know which room will be occupied and until when. Ask to review the order of areas and availability for any schedule change. If the event creates extra work, explain what you need rather than assuming it is part of the usual visit.

There are confidential documents on desks. What should we do before the visit?

Secure documents and clear only the surfaces you want cleaned. Identify restricted areas and agree which objects should not be moved. Papers left on a desk should not be confused with waste; give clear instructions to the service contact.

We noticed a missed detail when opening the business. How do we report it?

Send La Roca a photo of the specific spot, its location and the visit date. Explain when you noticed it and whether the space was used after cleaning. This helps review what happened against the agreed tasks and determine the next step.

Our headcount is increasing. Do we need to change the whole plan?

First identify what changes: occupied desks, restroom use, meals in the kitchenette or foot traffic. Share those details to review tasks and frequency for affected areas. A change in occupancy may require a revised proposal even when square footage stays the same.

The building already cleans the hallways. Can we book only our office?

Use a floor plan or walkthrough to identify your business areas and those covered by building management. Include shared entrances and restrooms where relevant. This helps request a proposal for the areas you need without overlapping responsibilities.

How do we distinguish waste, recycling and boxes we want to keep?

Identify bins and the building’s authorized disposal route. Mark boxes and materials that should be kept and communicate any special separation instructions. Agreeing what gets removed helps keep useful items and documents out of the waste stream.
