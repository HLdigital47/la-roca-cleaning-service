SERVICE SCOPE

What gets cleaned after a renovation

Construction dust reaches beyond the floor. We review accessible surfaces in each area to define cleaning that fits the actual condition of your property.

Surfaces and details

Dust on shelves, baseboards, door frames and accessible surfaces. Include nearby rooms affected by the renovation.

Kitchens and cabinets

Counters, sinks, cabinet fronts and empty shelves within the agreed scope. Flag residue on new finishes before trying to remove it.

Renovated bathrooms

Sinks, faucets, mirrors, toilets and shower or tub surfaces. Care follows the material and installation instructions.

Floors and walkways

Dust removal and cleaning compatible with the flooring. Consider entrances, stairs and routes used by workers.

Accessible interior glass

Review dust and marks on glass, frames and interior sills. Confirm the number of windows and any special access needs.

Residue requiring assessment

Paint, tape, adhesive and grout haze are identified before deciding on treatment. Assessment helps distinguish residue from finish damage.

A quote that reflects your project

Square footage helps, but it does not tell the whole story. The renovation type, dust buildup, new materials and access determine the work involved.

Before scheduling

Cleaning finished surfaces

MATERIAL CARE

New finishes: keep their care instructions handy

Ask your installer for the material name and cleaning instructions. A surface may look ready while still needing time before exposure to moisture or cleaning products.

Newly installed floors

Share the flooring type and installation or finish date. Keep the manufacturer’s instructions available to select compatible care.

Stone, glass and fixtures

Mention marble, natural stone, specialty coatings or new glass. Residue needs assessment before using abrasives or scraping.

Partial renovation handover

Identify protected rooms and rooms that stayed in use. Include hallways and entrances connected to the work area.

Questions before a project handover

Dust returned the next day. Does that mean it was not cleaned properly?

First check whether work continued in another room, materials were moved or dust entered from adjoining areas. Compare affected areas with the agreed scope and share photos with La Roca. Identifying the source helps determine which area needs further attention.

There are white marks on the newly installed tile. Can cleaning remove them?

White marks that remain after loose dust is removed may need a different assessment. Share a photo and the tile, grout or sealer type with your installer and La Roca. Avoid applying a remover before identifying the material and its care instructions.

Does surface cleaning also address dust inside the air ducts?

Cleaning accessible surfaces is different from cleaning inside ducts or HVAC equipment. If dust appears to come from vents, ask your contractor or an HVAC professional to check its source and the system’s condition before handover.

I found scratches or a stain after the dust was removed. What should I do?

Photograph the detail in good light and note its location before trying products or polishing. Share it with the installer to distinguish adhered dirt from a finish defect or damage. That distinction helps identify who should address it.

My contractor changed the completion date. How should I handle cleaning?

Tell La Roca as soon as you have a revised date and explain which tasks remain. Ask to review availability before confirming movers or key handover. If only part of the property needs to be used, identify which areas can be completed and made accessible.

Should furniture arrive before post-construction cleaning?

Where possible, keep the areas being cleaned clear to allow access to floors and details. If furniture is already present, share photos and identify pieces in the work area so the scope can be defined around them and any moving coordinated with the property’s responsible person.
