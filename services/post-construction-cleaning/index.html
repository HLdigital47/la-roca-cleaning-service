Before booking post-construction cleaning

When should the final cleaning be scheduled after a remodel?

Plan for after dust-producing work, repairs and contractor cleanup are complete. Paint, sealers and new flooring must be ready for their prescribed care. Keep water, power and access available, and leave time before the walkthrough or move-in for a final condition review.

Why is post-construction cleaning different from a deep clean?

Construction dust can reach cabinet interiors, trim, ledges and neighboring rooms, while a residential deep clean addresses household buildup. Explain the work performed, surfaces installed and remaining residue. The estimate should reflect the project condition rather than treating it as ordinary dusting.

Does the service include hauling debris and scraping off paint or adhesive?

Those needs require individual review. Removing debris is different from cleaning finished surfaces, and scraping or strong solvents can damage new materials. Identify the residue and material and clarify the contractor's responsibilities before agreeing on the final cleaning scope.

Will one cleaning stay dust-free if workers return afterward?

Further sanding, cutting or repairs can leave new dust on cleaned surfaces. Coordinate the sequence with the contractor and identify any remaining punch-list work. If workers return after the final clean, reassess the affected areas rather than assuming the original visit covers another cleanup.
