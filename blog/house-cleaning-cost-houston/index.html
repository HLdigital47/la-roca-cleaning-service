Choose the pricing model

An hourly package is useful when you want to prioritize a limited list of tasks. A service-based estimate works better when the whole scope can be described in advance. Ask whether the quote is based on team hours or individual cleaner hours before booking.

What changes the quote?

Room count, bathrooms, condition, pets, access and additional tasks all affect the work involved. Interior appliances and cabinets may require separate agreement. Review the current estimates on our pricing page; they are not fixed rates.

Making sense of cleaning estimates

Can I use a price someone posted online as the cost for my house?

Use it as one person's experience, not a quote. Their home, date, cleaner, frequency and tasks may differ from yours. Ask for the actual scope behind a number before comparing it with a professional estimate for your property.

How can two cleaning visits cost the same but offer different value?

One may cover routine floors and bathrooms while another includes more detailed work or appliance interiors. Visit length and team size also differ. Compare the room-by-room task list, first-visit condition and exclusions rather than judging value solely by the total amount.

Does more frequent cleaning always mean a lower monthly bill?

No. Even if a provider offers a lower price per visit, more visits can increase the monthly total. Compare price per appointment, expected number of visits and included tasks. Do not assume a recurring discount applies until it is part of your actual quote.

What questions should I ask before accepting a cleaning quote?

Ask which rooms and details are included, whether the price is for an initial or maintenance visit, how extras are handled and what booking terms apply. Confirm the final scope before work begins and keep a copy for your own review afterward.
